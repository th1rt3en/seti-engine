# Official FAQ (BGG, version 11.2024)

Source: `docs/rules/seti-faq-printer-friendly.pdf` (printable version of the official FAQ, version BGG 11.2024: boardgamegeek.com/thread/3392878). Each entry below restates one answer and points to the rule it affects. Where the FAQ adds a rule, the engine follows the FAQ. Where it only confirms the rulebook, the rule it confirms is named. `Q` numbers are the FAQ's own.

## Effects and timing

- **FAQ-01** (Q1) Effects are mandatory except where the effect itself offers a choice. The player may choose not to move a probe when they gain a movement, and not to tuck a card when they gain an income increase. Effects that cannot be skipped: rotating the solar system, marking a signal, taking a tech, gaining resources (including cards and data), scoring points, marking a trace (a 3-point overflow space is always available), and performing a main action when a card tells the player to. Resolving reached milestones is mandatory.
- **FAQ-02** (Q2) An effect that cannot be resolved (no card to tuck, no probe to move, at the probe limit when told to launch, all four techs of a color already owned) is skipped, and the card's other effects are still resolved. Example: the solar system still rotates when the tech cannot be taken.
- **FAQ-03** (Q3) Effects on a card resolve from left to right. When a tech is taken the solar system is rotated first (TECHACT-02). The text on the bottom part of a card says how the effects above it are resolved.
- **FAQ-04** (Q4) Free actions are performed only during the player's own turn, before, after or during the main action. A free action may interrupt a main action but a free action cannot be interrupted by another free action: it must be resolved completely first. Example: placing data on the 4th computer space triggers the income tuck, which must be fully resolved before further data is placed.
- **FAQ-05** (Q47) Researching a tech rotates the solar system once. The rotation icon on a card that also gives a tech is only a reminder.
- **FAQ-06** (Q46) There is no hand limit during a round; the player discards down to 4 only when passing (PASS-02). Every card can be discarded for its free-action corner effect.
- **FAQ-07** (Q32) Milestones are resolved at the end of the turn in which they are reached. No free action can be played during or after resolving them. This applies to all milestones, including those from alien species. With several players, in order starting with the player whose turn it was, clockwise (MILE-01, MILE-10).

## Actions and the board

- **FAQ-10** (Q5, Q16, Q17) An orbiter or lander never returns to the solar system board and an orbiter cannot land. Only a figure on the solar system board is a probe. Orbiters and landers are not probes for any effect.
- **FAQ-11** (Q6, Q7) The landing discount for an orbiter at the planet applies to any player's orbiter and to landing on that planet's moons. The landing-cost tech discount applies to moons too.
- **FAQ-12** (Q8) The marker placed beside a star when a sector is won is not a signal; it only records the win and covers the better reward (SECTOR-14).
- **FAQ-13** (Q9) Marking a signal is mandatory and always possible: if the data slot is full, the signal is an excess signal (no data gained, SECTOR-05).
- **FAQ-14** (Q10, Q11) A sector is a wedge from the Sun to the edge. A sector's star system is the one the ring spaces are currently aligned with, so "the sector with Earth" is the star system aligned with Earth's current position.
- **FAQ-15** (Q12) The card row is refilled after the whole scan action. Cards that mark two or three signals by discarding row cards discard all of them before the row is refilled (SCAN-03). The face-up alien card beside an alien deck cannot be discarded for a signal; other alien cards in hand can, if the player has a way to discard a card for a signal. Exertian cards cannot be discarded by any means (Q40).
- **FAQ-16** (Q13) A tie for the most markers in a sector is won by the player whose marker was placed later (SECTOR-11).
- **FAQ-17** (Q14, Q15) Analyze needs all 6 round spaces of the top row filled (computer techs add spaces whose bottom spaces need not be filled). The hexagonal analyze icon is not a space. Analyze removes all data from the top row and from all computer tech spaces, and nothing from the data pool. This is the only way to remove data from the computer (ANALYZE-01, ANALYZE-02).
- **FAQ-18** (Q18) "Visit a planet" means moving a probe onto that planet's space. A probe already there when the card is played does not count. Orbiters and landers do not count as visiting.
- **FAQ-19** (Q19, Q20, Q21) A probe moves to any orthogonally adjacent space; it can never move into or through the Sun. Movement may be divided among probes. Visiting the same planet again in the same turn gives its publicity again.
- **FAQ-20** (Q22, Q23) A computer tech can go in any computer tech slot (blue techs have no fixed slot) including a slot that holds a data token, in which case it goes under the token; this does not count as placing data (TECHACT-05). It is usually best to fill the leftmost empty slot first.
- **FAQ-21** (Q24) The probe-limit tech raises the limit to 2 probes in space, but the Launch action still launches a single probe.
- **FAQ-22** (Q25) The telescope tech that lets a player launch a probe during a Scan costs only that 1 energy; the 2 credits of the Launch action are not paid. Launching at the base cost still costs 2 credits (TECH-TT04).

## Missions and scoring

- **FAQ-30** (Q26, Q27) Conditional missions depend on the state of the game; triggerable missions depend on an activity and do not trigger for things done before the card was played. Cards always refer to the player's own things unless they say otherwise.
- **FAQ-31** (Q28, Q29) Covering a triggerable mission space is optional; if skipped the player must trigger it again later. One trigger covers only one space, even when the effect matches several spaces or the trigger exists as a specific and a generic version (a certain trace and any trace, and so on).
- **FAQ-32** (Q50) A single action can mark both an objective task and a triggerable mission space (solo).
- **FAQ-33** (Q30, Q31) Reaching a neutral milestone places only one nonplayer marker; the other player placing the second marker when they reach it (A-009). The marker goes to the leftmost empty of the 6 discovery spaces, filling the left species' three first, then the right species' (A-008). With all six full no marker is placed. Nonplayer markers never go on overflow spaces or on species boards.
- **FAQ-34** (Q33) Gold tile "2 completed missions / end-of-game scoring cards": end-of-game scoring cards that scored 0 points still count; only completed missions count among missions (END-13).
- **FAQ-35** (Q34, Q35) Gold tiles about income count only the cards tucked under the starting income card, not the printed resources. The 3-types tile counts only credit, energy and random-card income; income of other types from alien species does not count. The credit/energy tile counts the type with the most cards (END-14, END-15).
- **FAQ-36** (Q36) Gold tile "sector win + orbiter/lander": each set needs one sector win and one orbiter or lander; the count is the smaller of (wins) and (orbiters + landers). All of the player's markers count, even several in one sector, and landers on planets and moons both count (END-17).

## Alien species

- **FAQ-40** (Q37) At discovery only the players who marked one of the 3 discovery spaces are rewarded; overflow markers get no reward.
- **FAQ-41** (Q38, Q39) Discovery spaces and overflow spaces count as traces marked for that species. Each species has its own overflow spaces. A trace may be marked on any unoccupied space of the matching color on either species board, on discovery spaces of species not yet discovered, or on an overflow space (LIFE-01, LIFE-04, ALIEN-DATA-10).
- **FAQ-42** (Q41, Q42) Exofossils cannot be traded through resource exchange and are worth nothing at the end of the game.
- **FAQ-43** (Q43) Centaurian cards are not missions.
- **FAQ-44** (Q44, Q45) Exertian cards are neither missions nor end-of-game scoring cards, so they do not count for the gold tile that scores those. An Exertian card is scored once even if its condition is met several times over.

## Solo mode

- **FAQ-50** (Q48) The rival does not use a starting income card and gains no starting credits, energy or cards. Every time the rival would increase income (usually when placing an orbiter) it advances 4 spaces on the progress track instead (SOLO-10, SOLO-11).
- **FAQ-51** (Q49) The rival marks only the first space of a gold tile. If the first space of every gold tile is occupied it marks nothing. It never scores gold tiles; it scores 5 VP for each objective the player did not complete (SOLO-15, SOLO-OBJ-07).
- **FAQ-52** (Q51) The rival marks a signal on the 'Oumuamua tile only with the signal icon that shows 'Oumuamua (S.17). Every other signal icon ignores the tile; if the sector contains the tile, the signal goes in that sector's star system.
- **FAQ-53** (S.15) Mascamites action card, second action: either Saturn within 4 moves or Jupiter within 5 moves is always reachable. When the rival lands on Saturn or Jupiter (or a moon), take 1 random sample token from that planet, without its reward, flip it face up and put it on the next dedicated blue space of the Mascamites board; from then on it can be marked like any blue space. If the planet has no tokens left, skip taking one.
- **FAQ-54** (S.16) Anomalies action card: find the color of the next anomaly to trigger (the nearest in the counter-clockwise direction from Earth) and who has the highest marked trace of that color. If it is not the rival, it marks the lowest available space of that color on the Anomalies board and gains that space's reward plus 3 VP, even if that still does not make it the leader. Otherwise it does the second action.
- **FAQ-55** (S.17) 'Oumuamua action card: the first action moves the probe to 'Oumuamua within 4 moves and lands or orbits there. With no probe in space, or if it cannot be reached within 4 moves, the rival does the second action; its third signal (the one with the 'Oumuamua icon) is marked on the 'Oumuamua tile.
- **FAQ-56** (S.18) Centaurians action card: when the species is discovered the rival also takes the two unused message tiles, so it has three. The first action is done only if the rival has no message tile on the scoring track (it must reach the one on the track first); when all three have been placed, skip it. Then it advances on the progress track.
- **FAQ-57** (S.19) Exertians action card: the rival never holds Exertian cards. The first action is done only if (Exertian cards the rival played) + (traces it marked on the Exertian board that give danger, not counting discovery or overflow spaces) is less than 5; it then plays a random Exertian card face down from the deck.

## Cards

Card clarifications from pp. 12 to 26 of the FAQ. They are rules for those cards and go into the card definitions. Unless noted, "free-action corner" means the top-left corner and "discard for a signal" means a signal in a sector matching the color in the top-right corner.

- **FAQ-C001** Missions #1 to #4 (visit Jupiter/Saturn, Venus/Mercury, Uranus/Neptune, Venus/Jupiter): playing the card is a main action even at cost 0. A probe already on a required planet when the card is played does not count; it must move there again. An orbiter or lander does not count as visiting.
- **FAQ-C009** #9 Falcon Heavy: its two launches ignore the probe limit, so the player can end with 3 (or 4 with the probe-limit tech) probes in space. The limit still applies to later regular launches.
- **FAQ-C011** #11 Grant: draw from the row or a random card from the deck, reveal it and gain its free-action corner effect.
- **FAQ-C015** #15 Atmospheric Entry: when the orbiter on the 1st space (the extra 3 VP) is removed, that space is available again; the other orbiters do not slide, so the next orbiter there gains the 3 VP.
- **FAQ-C016** #16 Dragonfly: may land on an occupied space and still get the covered reward (the first-lander data too); it also allows landing on an occupied moon (with the moon tech).
- **FAQ-C017** #17 OSIRIS-REx: the chosen probe need not be on asteroids; it gains 1 data for each adjacent asteroid space.
- **FAQ-C019** #19 Gravitational Slingshot: applies to each planet visited later in the turn, including by free-action movement; the planet the probe is on when the card is played does not count.
- **FAQ-C020** #20 to #24 flybys: only visits made after the card is played count, and the points are gained once per turn even if the planet is visited several times.
- **FAQ-C025** #25 Lightsail: 1 point per unique planet visited this turn (Earth included); a planet counts once.
- **FAQ-C026** #26 Through the Asteroid Belt: leaving asteroids costs only 1 movement this turn, as with the tech.
- **FAQ-C027** #27 to #29 telescopes: resolve the movement first. The probe must be on the solar system board.
- **FAQ-C030** #30 Great Observatories Project: up to 3 probes of any players, each marks a signal in its sector; probes must be on the solar system board.
- **FAQ-C045** #45 to #47 and #65: resolve both signals by discarding the row cards first, then refill the row; for #65 only then rotate the solar system and gain the tech, so a tech bonus card can come from the refilled row.
- **FAQ-C050** #50 Square Kilometre Array: discard all three row cards for signals first, then refill; up to 6 points for three unique sectors.
- **FAQ-C051** #51 to #55 (scan cards): the base cost of the scan is not paid but additional costs of telescope techs are. #135 and #ET.14, ET.21, ET.22 are the same.
- **FAQ-C058** #58 Uranus Orbiter and Probe and #60 Trident Probe: the mission condition includes the planet's moon (the printed card omits it). #112 Planetary Geologic Mapping: a lander on the planet's moon counts too.
- **FAQ-C067** #67 Yevpatoria and #114 Planet Hunters: discarding cards for signals from hand follows the sector color of each card.
- **FAQ-C073** #73 Clean Space Initiative / #74 Pre-launch Testing: if not all free-action corners can be used the three row cards are still discarded.
- **FAQ-C078** #78 to #80 missions: when the action is performed, only one reward of the player's choice is marked; the spaces can be covered in any order.
- **FAQ-C081** #81 International Collaboration: the tech is one another player already has, the solar system is not rotated, and the tile's 3 VP, publicity, energy and card bonuses are not gained, but the free probe launch and the 2 data of the two particular techs are.
- **FAQ-C084** #84 Sample Return: as #15, removing the lander from the 1st space (2 data) frees that space again.
- **FAQ-C088** #88 Chandra and #134 Herschel: the 4 signals in different sectors must be present on the board at completion; signals removed earlier do not count. The two signals marked by the card can complete the mission before the sector they might complete is resolved.
- **FAQ-C089** #89 NIAC Program: Exertian cards are not in hand.
- **FAQ-C091** #91 to #93 income cards: first gain the rewards for cards already tucked, then tuck this card (an income increase, so its resource is gained immediately).
- **FAQ-C098** #98 to #100: discovery and overflow spaces count as spaces for the species.
- **FAQ-C101** #101 Telescope Time Allocation: for the scan, only one reward of the player's choice; if the scan completes a sector the reward and its extra signal can be taken before the sector is resolved.
- **FAQ-C103** #103 Westerbork: the same sector must be won twice.
- **FAQ-C106** #106 Strategic Planning: triggers only when a card costing 1, 2 or 3 credits is played as the main action (not discarded for its corner).
- **FAQ-C107** #107, #ET.13, #ET.29: only one reward of the player's choice per trigger (blue trace, tech, trace).
- **FAQ-C113** #113 Solvay Conference: score the gold tile the player did not mark as if on its lowest (rightmost) tier; if fewer than 3 tiles were marked, the player chooses among the unmarked ones.
- **FAQ-C116** #116 Control Center: several rewards can be triggered by one scan if the signals are in sectors of the indicated colors; earlier signals cannot trigger. The reward may be skipped and saved.
- **FAQ-C117** #117 Lunar Gateway: only one reward per orbit or land action.
- **FAQ-C118** #118 PLATO and #136 Algonquin: markers replace the data as usual but the data goes to the supply, not to the player.
- **FAQ-C119** #119 PIXL: because free actions may interrupt a main action, the player can take the blue tech with the 2-publicity bottom space, place data to gain publicity, and score extra from the card text.
- **FAQ-C120** #120 Orbiting Lagrange Point: the probe must be on the solar system board; to return the card to hand, the player must have no marker in that sector's data slot before the card is played; it can still return to hand if the signal completes the sector.
- **FAQ-C122** #122 Amateur Astronomers: three cards from the top of the deck, one at a time; every signal is mandatory.
- **FAQ-C123** #123 Asteroids Flyby, #124 Cometary Encounter, #129 Asteroid Research: playing is a main action even at cost 0; only visits after the card is played count; the data or points are gained once per turn (for #129 each visit may cover a reward, but only on the player's own turn, and a push by the player's own rotation counts).
- **FAQ-C125** #125 Trajectory Correction: "within the same ring" means a move that does not change the distance from the Sun.
- **FAQ-C126** #126 Euclid: choose an orange or a pink tech; the solar system rotates once.
- **FAQ-C127** #127 NEAR Shoemaker: the 13 points need a probe on asteroids at game end; another player's rotation can push it away.
- **FAQ-C128** #128 Advanced Navigation System: one reward per visit; several rewards for the same planet need several visits.
- **FAQ-C133** #133 Optimal Launch Window: 1 move for each other planet or comet in the three spaces lined up with Earth in the direction away from the Sun (up to 3).
- **FAQ-C138** #138 Cornell University: any free-action corner effect that includes 1 publicity, data or move qualifies, including stronger alien-card corners.
- **FAQ-C201** Mascamite cards ET.1 to ET.7: they can be used to land (or orbit for Mass Sample Collection) on any planet, but a sample can be picked up only on Jupiter or Saturn (or their moons) while tokens remain. A sample capsule must be on the destination space (Earth, or Mars for the Martian Quarantine Lab) to be delivered, and delivery can happen any time later as a free action. ET.7 gives the delivered reward twice. ET.5 needs the probe on Jupiter's or Saturn's space (not an orbiter or lander) and the chosen sample is shown and put back.
- **FAQ-C215** #ET.15 Part of Everyday Life: draw 3 random cards, then immediately choose one, discard it and gain its free-action corner effect; discard another and gain the resource of its income corner; keep the third.
- **FAQ-C220** #ET.20 Amazing Uncertainty: points for every signal the player has in the three sectors with an anomaly, including the one just marked; if it completes a sector, gain the points first, then resolve the sector.
- **FAQ-C221** #ET.21 Visitor in the Sky: the mission needs a marked space on the 'Oumuamua board that costs payment (a 25-VP space costing 4 exofossils or a top 6-VP space costing 1 exofossil); the 2 VP are for signals marked on the tile by the card. #ET.22: the exofossil is gained for marking at least one signal on the tile. #ET.23 and #ET.27: a signal in the 'Oumuamua sector can be marked in the star system or on the tile; "a signal on 'Oumuamua" means the marker on the tile; visiting 'Oumuamua works as visiting a planet.
- **FAQ-C231** Centaurian income cards (#ET.31 to ET.33, ET.38 to ET.40): when the delayed income is increased the resource is gained at once; they can also be tucked with the regular income effect; publicity and data incomes do not count for gold tiles that only count credit, energy and random-card incomes.
- **FAQ-C300** Promo #SE EN 01 Not a Planet Since 2006: Pluto has no space on the solar system board and cannot be visited. A probe on any space of the outermost ring can use an Orbit or Land action to become an orbiter or lander at Pluto, moving from the board to the card. Only the card's owner can use it. Orbit costs 1 credit and 1 energy, Land costs 3 energy, or 2 energy if the owner already has an orbiter at Pluto, reduced further by the landing tech. One orbiter and one lander at most. Pluto counts as a planet for other effects; its orbiter and lander count for the gold tile sector win + orbiter/lander and for missions that need an orbiter or lander. Cards #15 and #84 can remove them so Pluto can be used again; #16 Dragonfly allows a second landing.
